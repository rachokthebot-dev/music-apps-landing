Four apps · built for guitar players who actually practice

The practice tools I always wished existed.

I play guitar. I wanted software that respects the way musicians actually learn — slow passages down, loop the hard bits, steal licks from YouTube, feel the shape of a progression. So I built four apps. Here they are.

Shreddy

Learn any song. At your pace.

Drop in an MP3 or paste a YouTube URL. Shreddy splits the song into sections using AI — intro, verse, chorus, solo — then lets you loop the hard bits, slow them down without ruining the pitch, and track what you've practiced.

AI section detection

Upload a song and Shreddy marks the intro, verses, choruses, bridges, and solos automatically — so you can jump straight to the part you need to work on.

Loop the hard bits

Tap any section to loop it. Shift-tap to loop a range. Set custom A–B markers anywhere. Your loops survive reloads, so tomorrow's practice picks up where today's left off.

Slow down without going flat

Play back at 0.5x to 1.2x with no pitch change. Transpose up or down 12 semitones to match your voice or a different tuning.

Visual waveform timeline

See the whole song's structure at a glance. Color-coded sections. Tempo, key, and time signature are detected from the audio itself.

Built-in metronome

Locked to the song's beat. Count-in, tap tempo, volume. No need to switch apps mid-practice.

Practice stats that matter

Daily streaks, weekly minutes, most-practiced songs. Proof that you actually showed up — not just a number, a habit.

LickBank

Steal every lick worth stealing.

Every guitar player has a YouTube history full of "that one lick at 2:43." LickBank is where those moments finally stop getting lost. Paste a video, snip the exact phrase, file it under the song it came from.

Import from YouTube

Paste a URL. The video lands in your library with its thumbnail, title, and the moment frozen at the lick you care about.

Organized by source song

Color-coded cards group licks from the same song. Folders for "Pentatonic," "Rock," "Come Together" — however you think about your vocabulary.

Searchable, fast

Find the Man in the Box riff, the bridge solo from that Tears for Fears cover, the pentatonic run you bookmarked six months ago. All in one place.

ChordCraft

Feel the shape of a song.

Twenty progressions across blues, country, folk, jazz, pop, and rock. Hear each one in any key, with bass and drums behind it, with real chord diagrams in front of it. The quickest way to internalize how genres actually move.

Twenty progressions, every key

I–V–vi–IV, 12-bar blues, Pachelbel, power ballad, grunge, jazz turnarounds. Transpose to any key with one tap.

Bass and drums built in

Hear the progression in context — not just isolated chord tones. Mix the bass and drum levels to your taste.

Chord diagrams and voicings

Every chord shows real fretboard fingerings. Swap voicings, add 7ths, explore extended chords to hear how complexity changes the feel.

Metronome

Keep time. Nothing else.

The metronome I wanted on my iPad: one screen, huge numbers, dark when the lights are low. No ads, no subscriptions, no fifteen settings you'll never use. Just the click.

Tap tempo

Tap along to find the BPM of whatever's in your head. Works on touch, click, or keypress.

Time signatures that matter

4/4, 3/4, 6/8. Beat indicators at the top show you exactly where you are in the measure.

Practice timer

Set a fade-out timer for 1, 2, 5, or 10 minutes — or type your own. Puts a bound on practice blocks without breaking your flow.

Local-first web apps. Works on the iPad you already keep on the music stand.
